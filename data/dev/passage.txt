The committee's report, released late Tuesday, argues that the current framework for evaluating municipal infrastructure grants has drifted well beyond its original purpose. What began as a narrow mechanism for distributing federal repair funds to distressed counties has, over fifteen years, accumulated a thicket of eligibility criteria that few applicants can navigate without professional assistance. The authors note, with some understatement, that the median application now runs to four hundred pages. They recommend consolidating the seven existing programs into two, and shifting the burden of demonstrating need from the applicant to the administering agency, which already holds most of the relevant data. Critics of the proposal argue that such consolidation would strip away protections for the smallest jurisdictions, whose circumstances rarely fit standard templates. The committee acknowledges the concern but contends that the status quo already fails those communities, simply less visibly.
